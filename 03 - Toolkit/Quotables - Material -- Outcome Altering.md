Yes. You’re looking for **business-impact vocabulary** that sits on a spectrum from “worth noting” to “this meaningfully changes the outcome.”

### Best terms

|Term / phrase|Implication|
|---|---|
|**Relevant**|Should be included in the thinking|
|**Notable**|Worth calling attention to|
|**Meaningful**|Large enough to matter|
|**Non-trivial**|Not negligible, even if not huge|
|**Consequential**|Has real downstream effects|
|**Significant**|Important or sufficiently large|
|**Material**|Significant enough to affect a decision/conclusion|
|**Substantive**|Affects the substance, not just presentation/noise|
|**Impactful**|Produces a noticeable effect, though sometimes vague|
|**Decision-relevant**|Could influence the decision|
|**Decision-sensitive**|Decision may change depending on this factor|
|**Consideration point**|Deserves explicit consideration|
|**Risk factor**|Introduces potential downside|
|**Headwind**|Works against the desired outcome|
|**Downside pressure**|Pushes the result downward|
|**Downward pressure**|Likely suppresses the metric/outcome|
|**Adverse impact**|Negative effect|
|**Material downside**|Meaningful negative exposure|
|**Materially adverse**|Meaningfully negative|
|**Outcome-altering**|Strong enough to potentially change the conclusion|
|**Decision-altering**|Stronger still, could change the actual decision|

### For the exact thought you're expressing

Instead of:

> "It matters, it's a consideration point, it may have material downward impacts."

I'd probably use one of these:

**Neutral / analytical**

> This is a **meaningful consideration and may exert downward pressure on the result.**

**Executive**

> This is a **material consideration with potential downside impact.**

**More cautious**

> This is a **relevant consideration that could negatively influence the outcome.**

**More forceful**

> This represents a **material downside risk** to the expected outcome.

**Very BI / analysis-oriented**

> This factor is **decision-relevant and directionally negative.**

### Useful scale

For the kind of BI/executive communication you do, I'd mentally organize these as:

worth knowing

    ↓

relevant

    ↓

notable

    ↓

meaningful

    ↓

significant

    ↓

material

    ↓

consequential

    ↓

decision-altering

And separately, describe **direction**:

positive   → tailwind / upside / favorable impact

negative   → headwind / downside / adverse impact

neutral    → limited / negligible / immaterial impact

So you can compose phrases very efficiently:

- **meaningful headwind**
- **material downside risk**
- **significant downward pressure**
- **non-trivial consideration**
- **decision-relevant factor**
- **potentially consequential impact**
- **substantive adverse effect**
- **materially unfavorable**
- **meaningful downside exposure**

For your style, I especially like **meaningful**, **material**, **consequential**, **decision-relevant**, **headwind**, and **downside pressure** because each carries a slightly different and useful semantic meaning.
