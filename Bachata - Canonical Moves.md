In Bachata, the way you hold your partner's hands—the **connection**—completely changes which moves are possible. Beyond the standard "closed" or "open" positions, the hand-to-hand configuration dictates the tension and the "flow" of the dance.

Here is a breakdown of **Cross-Hand** versus the other most common hand configurations in Sensual and Modern Bachata:

### 1. Cross-Hand (The X-Grip)

In this position, the leader’s right hand holds the follower’s right hand, and the leader’s left holds the follower’s left, creating an "X" shape.

- **Best for:** Wraps, "cuddles," and complex "pretzel" turns.
    
- **The Feel:** This is a high-control position. Because your arms are crossed, you can lead the follower into a wrap (where they end up in front of you with their arms around themselves) very easily.
    
- **Performance Tip:** Since you are preparing for a performance, this is a great position for "unwrapping" into a dramatic dip or a hair flick.
    

### 2. Parallel Hold (The Standard Open)

This is the "standard" open position where the leader’s left hand holds the follower’s right, and the leader’s right holds the follower’s left.

- **Best for:** Basic turns, simple body waves, and preparing to move into a closed position.
    
- **The Feel:** This offers the most freedom of movement. It is the "neutral" ground of bachata.
    
- **The Transition:** Most dancers start here and then "switch" one hand to enter a cross-hand grip.
    

### 3. One-Handed Lead (The "Single")

The leader drops one hand and leads only with the other (usually the leader’s left to follower’s right).

- **Best for:** Tension-based moves like "The Hammerlock" or preparing for the **Neck Pass** you mentioned earlier.
    
- **The Feel:** It feels more spacious. It allows the follower to perform more individual "styling" or footwork while still being connected.
    
- **Performance Use:** This is often used right before the leader places a hand on the follower’s shoulder to initiate a neck roll or a body wave.
    

### 4. Over-Under (The Hammerlock Prep)

One hand is held high (around head height) and the other is held low (around waist height).

- **Best for:** Leading the follower into a turn where one arm ends up behind their back.
    
- **The Feel:** This creates a "gate" for the follower to walk through. It is a very common way to transition into "Shadow Position" (where the follower is in front of the leader, both facing the same way).

### How this relates to your Neck Pass

For the specific **neck roll under and through** you’re working on, dancers often start in a **One-Handed Lead**. The leader uses the free hand to reach for the shoulder, then releases the "held" hand to allow the follower's body to move freely through the roll.

Alternatively, if you are in a **Cross-Hand** grip, you can lead a "wrap," and then perform the neck pass while the follower is in the "cuddle" position for a very compressed, high-energy look.
