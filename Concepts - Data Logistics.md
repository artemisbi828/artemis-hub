Finding the right terminology usually depends on the "vibe" of your workflow—whether you want it to feel like a high-tech data pipeline, a clean kitchen, or a professional archive.

Here are a few sets of words categorized by the "energy" they bring to your system:

### 1. The "Data Pipeline" (Technical & Precise)

This is best if you want your folders to feel like a functional machine.

* **Ingest / Process:** Taking the raw file in.
* **Refine / Extract:** Distilling the core information.
* **Deploy / Commit:** Moving the "refined" version to its final home.
* **Deprecate / Vault:** Moving the original to a hidden archive.

### 2. The "Culinary/Artisan" (Creative & Manual)

If your notes feel more like raw materials that you are crafting into something better.

* **Harvest:** Gathering the initial file.
* **Reduce / Distill:** Getting to the "essence" of the content.
* **File / Shelve:** Moving it to its permanent location.
* **Compost:** This is a great word for "deleting" or "archiving"—the original is gone from sight but its "nutrients" (info) are now in your main system.

### 3. The "Library/Curatorial" (Organized & Formal)

Great for a system focused on long-term knowledge management (like a Second Brain).

* **Acquisition:** Bringing the file into the system.
* **Synthesize / Abstract:** Summarizing and reducing the content.
* **Catalog:** Placing the processed version in the right folder.
* **Cold Storage:** Moving the original to a hidden, non-indexed folder for "just in case" rollbacks.

### 4. Comparison Table: Action vs. Archive

If you are naming folders or tags, these pairings work well together:

| Process Step | Action Word | Archive/Hide Word |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Distill** | Condense, Parse, Atomize | **Legacy**, Ghost, Background |
| **Move** | Relocate, Migrate, Graduate | **Shadow**, Attic, Deep Storage |
| **Delete/Hide** | Prune, Purge, Evict | **Outbox**, Baseline, History |

---

### Implementation Tip: The "Shadow" Folder

If you want to "hide from view" without actually deleting, many Obsidian users create a folder named `.Archive` (starting with a period).

* **The Perk:** Most operating systems and some Obsidian plugins treat folders starting with a `.` as hidden or low-priority.
* **The Rollback:** You can still use the global search to find things in there if you're desperate, but they won't clutter your daily navigation.
