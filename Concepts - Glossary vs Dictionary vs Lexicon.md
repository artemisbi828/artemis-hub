---
definition: distinct classifications for organized terminology based on scope, depth, and structural intent
---
> [!info] Analogy: The Toolbox vs. The Hardware Store
> 
> A **Glossary** is the specific instruction manual for a single IKEA desk. A **Dictionary** is the entire hardware store catalog. A **Lexicon** is the underlying language of carpentry itself.

### Structural Comparison

|**Feature**|**Glossary**|**Dictionary**|**Lexicon**|
|---|---|---|---|
|**Scope**|Project/Domain specific|General language|Linguistic/Mental set|
|**Depth**|Contextual definitions|Etymology, Phonetics, Usage|Vocabulary inventory|
|**Primary Goal**|Clarity within a document|Universal standardization|Linguistic mapping|

---

### Core Attributes

- **Glossary** → **Contextual Filter**: Aggregates jargon or specialized terms found within a specific body of work to ensure stakeholder alignment.
    
- **Dictionary** → **Standardization Engine**: A comprehensive reference of a language's words, providing pronunciation, grammatical function, and historical evolution.
    
- **Lexicon** → **Cognitive Inventory**: In linguistics, the total "database" of words available to a speaker or a specific language branch; the "vocabulary" component of a grammar.
    

```
Knowledge Hierarchy
├── Dictionary (Macro: Universal Language)
│   └── Lexicon (Meso: Domain or Speaker Vocabulary)
│       └── Glossary (Micro: Document/Technical Specifics)
```
