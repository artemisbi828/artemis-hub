The liquid discussed in the video to attract, stimulate, and multiply microbes and worms is **Aerated Compost Tea (ACT)** [[15:03](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=YjXYW688WNk&t=903)]. Because you already have red wigglers, this microbial elixir is perfect. Applying it to your active worm bin will excite them and accelerate feeding, while applying it to your separate hot compost pile will introduce the vital microbes (bacteria and fungi) that serve as the primary food source that wild worms crave, drawing them to that area.

### Part 1: The Liquid Recipe & Brewing Guide

This recipe uses a 5-gallon brewing setup to create an aerobic, living microbial liquid [[15:53](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=YjXYW688WNk&t=953)].

#### **Ingredients & Materials**

- **5 Gallons of Clean Water:** Must be chlorine-free [[15:53](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=YjXYW688WNk&t=953)].
    
- **2 Cups of High-Quality Worm Castings:** Harvested from your red wigglers [[16:21](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=YjXYW688WNk&t=981)].
    
- **2 Tablespoons of Unsulfured Blackstrap Molasses:** Feeds and multiplies the bacteria [[16:28](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=YjXYW688WNk&t=988)].
    
- **1 Spoonful of Liquid Kelp (or Fish Hydrolysate):** Feeds and explodes the fungal population [[16:35](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=YjXYW688WNk&t=995)].
    
- **Filter Bag:** An old pillowcase, cheesecloth, or mesh paint strainer bag [[16:21](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=YjXYW688WNk&t=981)].
    
- **Aeration System:** An ordinary aquarium air pump with an air stone [[16:45](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=YjXYW688WNk&t=1005)].
    

#### **Brewing Steps**

1. **Prepare the Water [[15:53](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=YjXYW688WNk&t=953)]:** If using municipal tap water, you must de-chlorinate it first. Either leave the 5-gallon bucket of water sitting open for 24 hours to let the chlorine evaporate [[16:06](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=YjXYW688WNk&t=966)], or crush a single **Vitamin C tablet** into the water to neutralize it in about 15 minutes [[16:13](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=YjXYW688WNk&t=973)].
    
2. **Bag the Castings [[16:21](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=YjXYW688WNk&t=981)]:** Place 2 cups of your red wiggler worm castings into your filter bag and tie it closed. Suspend or place the bag into the water.
    
3. **Add the Food [[16:28](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=YjXYW688WNk&t=988)]:** Stir in the 2 tablespoons of unsulfured blackstrap molasses and the spoonful of liquid kelp/fish hydrolysate [[16:35](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=YjXYW688WNk&t=995)].
    
4. **Oxygenate [[16:45](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=YjXYW688WNk&t=1005)]:** Drop the aquarium air stone to the bottom of the bucket and turn on the air pump. _Crucial: The pump must run continuously to keep the brew aerobic. Without oxygen, the liquid turns into a foul, anaerobic sludge [[16:56](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=YjXYW688WNk&t=1016)]._
    
5. **Brewing Time [[17:13](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=YjXYW688WNk&t=1033)]:** Let the mixture bubble for **24 to 36 hours**.
    
    - **The Nose Test [[17:01](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=YjXYW688WNk&t=1021)]:** Check the smell. A successful brew should smell sweet and earthy [[17:04](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=YjXYW688WNk&t=1024)]. If it smells sour, rotten, or like ammonia, discard it and start over [[17:08](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=YjXYW688WNk&t=1028)].
        

### Part 2: Operator Runbook for Worm Attraction & Excitement

Because this liquid is teeming with live aerobic organisms, **it has a short shelf life of 4 to 6 hours** once you turn off the air pump [[17:17](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=YjXYW688WNk&t=1037)]. You must apply it immediately.

#### **Use Case A: Excite Your Existing Red Wiggler Bin**

Red wigglers do not actually eat raw food scraps directly; they consume the rotting organic matter and the _microbes_ coating the decomposing food. Pouring this microbial liquid into their bin acts as an immediate stimulant.

- **Application Protocol:**
    
    1. Once the brew is finished, turn off the pump and gently pour the aerated tea over your worm bin bedding and food feeding zones.
        
    2. Do not oversaturate the bin to the point of waterlogging (it should remain like a wrung-out sponge).
        
    3. **Why it works:** It pre-colonizes the bedding and food with billions of beneficial bacteria and fungi. This makes the environment immediately "tasty" and highly active, causing your red wigglers to congregate and feed aggressively.
        

#### **Use Case B: Attract Wild Worms to Your Separate Hot Compost Pile**

While hot compost piles naturally heat up and break down thermophilic organic matter, they can sometimes lack the fungal and ecological networks that earthworms desire. Adding compost tea inoculates the pile with the exact biology worms track down.

- **Application Protocol:**
    
    1. Wait until your hot compost pile has completed its initial high-heat thermophilic phase and has started to cool down (worms will flee temperatures above 95°F/35°C).
        
    2. Drench the outer edges and the base where the pile meets the native soil with the fresh aerated compost tea.
        
    3. **Why it works:** The liquid delivers a massive dose of active fungi (including _Trichoderma_ and mycorrhizal companions) [[12:06](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=YjXYW688WNk&t=726), [12:26](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=YjXYW688WNk&t=746)] and bacteria. These organisms release rich odors and produce sticky proteins like _glomalin_ [[13:10](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=YjXYW688WNk&t=790)]. Native earthworms and nightcrawlers in the surrounding soil will sense this thriving biological activity and migrate directly into the base of your pile to feed.
