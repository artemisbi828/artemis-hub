# Spider Mites
Spider mite eggs hatch every 3 to 7 days depending on the temperature. If you only treat once, the next generation will emerge and restart the colony.

- **Frequency:** Treat the plant every **4–5 days** for at least three consecutive cycles.
    
- **Environment:** Keep the humidity around the plant high. Grouping your plants or using a humidifier makes the environment "hospitable" for the plant but "hostile" for the mites.

### 1. The Physical Blast (The "Knockdown")

Before applying any chemicals, take the plant to a shower or use a garden hose.

- **Action:** Spray the undersides of the leaves with a firm stream of water.
    
- **Why:** This physically dislodges the mites and destroys their delicate webbing. Since mites love dry air, increasing the humidity immediately slows their life cycle.
    

### 2. The Suffocation (The Neem Oil Phase)

Neem oil is effective against mites because it coats them and prevents them from breathing, while also disrupting their hormones.

- **The Mix:** Use 1–2 teaspoons of pure neem oil and 1 teaspoon of mild Castile soap (like Dr. Bronner’s) per liter of warm water.
    
- **Application:** You must coat every square inch, especially the undersides of the leaves.
    
- **Caution:** Never apply neem oil in direct sunlight, as it will "cook" the leaves. Do it in the evening or a shaded spot.
    

### 3. The Equivalent to "Alcohol": Insecticidal Soap or Miticide

While alcohol works for mealybugs, spider mites are often too numerous for spot-treating with a swab.

- **Better Alternative:** Use a dedicated **Insecticidal Soap** or a **Horticultural Oil**. These are safer for the plant's cuticle than high-strength rubbing alcohol but just as deadly to the mites.
    
- **For stubborn cases:** Look for products containing **Spinosad** or **Abamectin**. These are the "heavy hitters" that function similarly to how bromide treatments work for larger pests.
