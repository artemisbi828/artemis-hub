### TL;DR (executive-safe)

**If governance costs more effort than it saves, it will be bypassed.  
Effective governance = low friction, high signal, measurable value.**

---

### One-liner options (pick your tone)

- **“Governance must lower friction, not add it.”**
- **“If it’s hard to follow, it won’t be followed.”**
- **“Adoption is the KPI—complexity kills adoption.”**
- **“Governance should feel like a guardrail, not a gate.”**
- **“Net value positive or it’s net ignored.”**
- **“The cost of compliance cannot exceed the cost of chaos.”**
- **“Simple scales. Complex stalls.”**

---

### Executive framing (buzzword-dense)

- **Frictionless Compliance** → Minimize user effort to maximize adherence
- **Adoption-First Design** → Measure success by actual usage, not policy coverage
- **Value-over-Process** → Every control must have a positive cost-benefit
- **Right-Sized Controls** → Calibrate rigor to risk (no blanket over-engineering)
- **Operational Throughput** → Governance should not bottleneck delivery
- **Guardrails, not Gatekeeping** → Enable speed with bounded safety
- **Standardize the 80%** → Keep edge-case complexity out of the core path

---

### Simple decision test (deterministic)

Use this as your executive litmus test:

```
For each governance control:
  If (User Effort > Risk Reduction Value)
     → Remove or simplify
  If (Time-to-Comply > Time-to-Bypass)
     → Redesign immediately
  If (Adoption Rate < 90%)
     → Over-engineered or misaligned
```

---

### Anti-patterns to explicitly discourage

- **“Policy-heavy, workflow-light”** → lots of rules, no usable path
- **Over-generalization** → designing for rare edge cases upfront
- **Serial approvals / multi-hop workflows** → latency with no added signal
- **Manual-first controls** → don’t scale, guarantee drift
- **Ambiguous ownership** → slows decisions, reduces accountability

---

### Closing punchline

**“Good governance disappears into the workflow. Bad governance becomes the workflow.”**
