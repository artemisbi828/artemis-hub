### The Compensation Hierarchy (Highest to Lowest Pay)

In terms of median total compensation (base + bonus + equity) in the U.S. tech and corporate market:

1. **Data Architect / Technical Product Owner (TPO / Technical PM)** *(Tie for top tier: ~$165,000 – $240,000+ total comp)*
*Data Architects command higher senior base floors due to rare systems-level mastery; Technical Product Owners often carry a higher equity ceiling in product-led tech companies.*
2. **DevOps Engineer / SRE** *(~$140,000 – $195,000 total comp)*
3. **Data Engineer** *(~$130,000 – $185,000 total comp)*
4. **PMO (Project / Program Management Office Lead)** *(~$115,000 – $165,000 total comp)*
5. **Power BI Developer / BI Developer** *(~$100,000 – $140,000 total comp)*
6. **Data Analyst** *(~$80,000 – $115,000 total comp)*

### TL;DR Role Descriptions (Material Differences Only)

| Role | Core Mission & Material Distinction (No Overlap) |
| --- | --- |
| **Data Architect** | **Blueprints the ecosystem.** Decides *how* enterprise data assets are structured, stored, and governed (e.g., Lakehouse vs. Data Mesh, bronze/silver/gold schema standards, enterprise security models). They do not build daily pipelines or reports; they dictate the technical standards others build within. |
| **Technical Product Owner (TPO)** | **Defines the "What" and "Why."** Owns the technical backlog, feature prioritization, and business trade-offs for engineering platforms (e.g., internally facing data platforms or APIs). They write user stories and manage stakeholder roadmaps, but write zero production pipeline code. |
| **DevOps / Platform Engineer** | **Builds the delivery machinery & uptime.** Automates CI/CD deployment pipelines, cloud provisioning (Terraform/IaC), container orchestration (Kubernetes), and infrastructure monitoring. They don't care what is inside the data; they care that code deploys smoothly and systems stay up. |
| **Data Engineer** | **Builds the plumbing.** Writes production-grade code (SQL, Python, Spark) to extract, clean, transform, and load raw data from source systems into analytical warehouses. They care about latency, pipeline resilience, batch/streaming jobs, and ingestion logic. |
| **PMO (Program/Project Manager)** | **Governs schedule, process, and budget.** Tracks velocity, cross-team dependencies, risk mitigation, and milestone delivery across multiple projects. They do not define technical architecture or prioritize feature value; they ensure work gets done predictably. |
| **Power BI Developer** | **Transforms warehouse tables into reporting layers.** Masters semantic data modeling (Star Schemas, Tabular Engine), DAX calculation logic, refresh scheduling, and visual UX/dashboards. Unlike the engineer who lands the data, they shape the analytical consumption model. |
| **Data Analyst** | **Extracts business narrative from existing data.** Queries clean datasets using SQL/Excel to answer ad-hoc business questions, run descriptive statistics, and provide operational recommendations. They consume models created by others rather than designing enterprise data infrastructure. |

### Your Transition Advantage

Because you already operate as a **BI Developer with Data Architect, Data Engineering, and Analytics experience**, your lowest-friction, highest-paying moves are:

1. **Analytics Engineer (The Sweet Spot):** Bridges Data Engineering and BI. Focuses on transformation modeling in warehouse layers (dbt, SQL, Snowflake/BigQuery) and semantic layers. Pay is significantly higher than BI Developer (`~$135k – $170k`).
2. **Data Architect:** If you already design schemas, evaluate modern data stack tools, and govern data contracts, transitioning your title directly to Data Architect will yield the single largest compensation jump without needing to become a pure software engineer.
3. **Technical Product Manager / Owner (Data Platform):** If you prefer driving stakeholder strategy, translating business requirements into technical specs, and managing data delivery roadmaps rather than writing DAX/pipelines all day.
