(Russian: doveryai, no proveryai) is an old Russian proverb famously adopted by U.S. President Ronald Reagan during the 1980s. Reagan learned the phrase from Suzanne Massie to facilitate nuclear arms treaty negotiations with Soviet General Secretary Mikhail Gorbachev, emphasizing a combination of good faith and necessary verification. 

Origin: It is a traditional Russian proverb, sometimes noted for being utilized in the early Soviet era by figures like Vladimir Lenin or Joseph Stalin.

Reagan’s Adoption: Reagan frequently used the phrase (in Russian: doveriai, no proveriai) during summits between 1984 and 1987, most notably regarding the 1987 Intermediate-Range Nuclear Forces (INF) Treaty.

Gorbachev's Response: During the INF treaty signing on December 8, 1987, Gorbachev noted, "You repeat that at every meeting," to which Reagan replied, "I like it".
Meaning: The phrase means that one should approach negotiations in good faith ("trust") but ensure compliance with agreements through strict monitoring ("verify").
